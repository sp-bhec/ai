Module 1: Defining Cosmic Habitability

Extremophiles: Challenging What We Know

Historically, scientists believed life could only exist in cozy temperatures with lots of sunlight and standard air pressure. Astrobiology changed this view with the discovery of extremophiles—organisms that survive and thrive under environments once thought impossible. If they can live there on Earth, why not on frozen Moons or hostile alien planets?

Module 2: Orbit Dynamics & Radiation

The Goldilocks Habitable Zone (HZ)

To harbor life as we understand it, a planet needs a reliable surface liquid solvent. Water is the most abundant and thermodynamically favorable choice. The Habitable Zone is the narrow shell around a star where the temperature is "just right" for liquid water to exist on an Earth-sized world.

Module 3: Probabilities & SETI

The Drake Equation: Deciphering the Odds

In 1961, astronomer Dr. Frank Drake designed a probabilistic formula to estimate $N$, the number of active, radio-communicating civilizations in our Milky Way galaxy. It isn't a law with a singular answer, but rather a scientific framework for discussing the hurdles of cosmic survival.

The Communicative Civilizations Formula
$$N = R_* \times f_p \times n_e \times f_l \times f_i \times f_c \times L$$

$R_*$: Star Formation Rate 10/yr

Average new stars per year in the Milky Way.

$f_p$: Fraction with Planets 50%

Percentage of stars hosting planetary systems.

$n_e$: Habitable Planets per Star 1.50

Worlds per stellar system that physically support life.

$f_l$: Fraction where Life Evolves 10%

Percentage of candidate planets where biology actually begins.

$f_i$: Fraction that Becomes Intelligent 10%

Percentage of biological worlds developing technological brains.

$f_c$: Fraction Communicative 10%

Percentage that releases radio signals into deep space.

$L$: Civilization Lifetime (Years) 5,000 yrs

Average length of time a technology-advanced race communicates before collapse or silence.
